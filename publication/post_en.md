Two days after the U.S. midterms, Democrats already hold a majority in the House, the Senate and the governorships.

I compared 2026 with the 2024 election and with the historical series. The 2026 numbers are still provisional, for comparison:

- House: 215 D vs 220 R in 2024; now 246 D vs 146 R, with 43 seats uncalled.
- Senate: 47 D vs 53 R before; now 53 D vs 44 R, with 3 uncalled.
- Governors: 24 D vs 26 R before; now 27 D vs 19 R, with 4 uncalled.
- The president's party almost always loses seats in a midterm. The biggest loss since 1946 was 64 seats, in 2010. Republicans now lose between 31 and 74.

Representation:
- Women in the House: 125 elected in 2024 and at least 124 in 2026. Democratic women went from 94 to 109; Republican women from 31 to 15.
- Hispanic Americans: from 49 to at least 50. Native Americans: from 3 to 4 in the House, and Peggy Flanagan will be the first Native American woman elected senator.
- LGBTQ+: from 12 to at least 13 in the House; Chris Pappas is the first openly gay man elected senator.
